Research

Modelling dependence.
Understanding uncertainty.

My research examines how variability evolves over time and interacts across locations and networks.

I develop and study statistical methods for dependent data, combining mathematical modelling, computational estimation and empirical analysis. My applications include financial networks and environmental time series.

Research themes

Spatiotemporal volatility

Models that combine temporal dynamics with spatial dependence to describe changing conditional variability. I am particularly interested in exponential GARCH formulations and asymmetric volatility spillovers.

Financial networks

The role of network structure in volatility modelling and forecasting, including comparisons between spatiotemporal and multivariate approaches.

Environmental applications

Spatiotemporal analysis of environmental variability, including wind-speed volatility and dependence across observation locations.

Estimation and forecasting

Likelihood-based estimation, numerical optimisation, Monte Carlo simulation and evaluation of predictive performance for dependent-data models.

Doctoral research

Asymmetric volatility spillovers

Exponential Spatiotemporal GARCH Model with Asymmetric Volatility Spillovers

My doctoral research at the University of Glasgow focused on an exponential spatiotemporal GARCH framework for modelling volatility and asymmetric spillovers across locations or network nodes.

The work combined statistical model development, quasi-maximum likelihood estimation, numerical optimisation and simulation studies.

Supervisors: Professor Philipp Otto and Professor Janine Illian.
